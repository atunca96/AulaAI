Audit the lesson as a publication editor, not as a stylist. Preserve correct content and repair only material defects. Every target-language sentence, example, dialogue turn, table cell and assessment item must be independently grammatical, idiomatic and complete for its intended function. Check required predicates, auxiliaries/copulas where the language uses them, argument structure and valency, agreement, case/adposition government, articles/determiners, particles/clitics, classifiers/counters, tense/aspect/mood, polarity, word order, reference, register and collocation. Reject malformed fragments unless the fragment itself is intentionally being taught.
Cross-check every rule against every example, translation, table, dialogue and assessment in the same lesson. A correct conclusion with a false explanation is still wrong. Do not classify a meaning under the wrong construction because a neighboring pattern looks similar. Keep person, number, gender where relevant, quantity, time, place, definiteness, role, referent and communicative force aligned across bilingual pairs. Reject literal calques, false friends, invented morphology or etymology, and explanations whose causal reasoning is false.
Treat rules with exact scope. Distinguish productive rules from common tendencies, restricted patterns, lexical conventions, irregular forms and exceptions. Words equivalent to always, never, only, must, impossible or without exception are allowed only when the stated scope is genuinely exceptionless. Cultural, pragmatic, regional, formal, colloquial, dated or dialectal tendencies must be scoped rather than universalized.
Pronunciation and writing-system claims must be context-sensitive and internally consistent. Do not collapse stress-conditioned, position-conditioned, neighboring-sound-conditioned or register-conditioned pronunciation into one universal substitution rule. Keep IPA, phonemic notation, transliteration, romanization and learner respelling explicitly distinguishable; pedagogical approximations are not exact phonetic truth. Preserve authentic script, diacritics, combining marks, punctuation, logical character order and visible symbols. For languages normally written in a non-Latin script, ordinary target-language examples and assessments use that script; transliteration may supplement it or be the explicit skill under test, but must not silently replace it.
Keep vocabulary-table columns on the same lexical item and sense. Keep dialogue roles, politeness, deixis and references coherent from turn to turn. Keep the instructional language consistent except where foreign material is intentionally being taught. Every MCQ must have exactly one defensible answer, four plausible distinct options, a key that is one option, and an explanation that supports that same answer. The decisive evidence must come from taught target-language knowledge rather than commonsense, arithmetic, stereotypes, trivia or category guessing.
Calibrate A1-A2 to high-frequency concrete language and transparent explanations, B1-B2 to productive contextual variation, and C1-C2 to fine register, pragmatics, idiom and discourse nuance. When a fine-grained claim cannot be made confidently, simplify or remove it rather than guessing.
