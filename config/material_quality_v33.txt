Apply these residual-error checks surgically: (1) instructional-language purity: explanatory text and translations must stay in the selected material language unless a foreign form is intentionally being taught; remove accidental English/Turkish leakage such as an untranslated gloss inside another locale. (2) symbol completeness: when naming a letter, grapheme, diacritic, punctuation mark, tone mark, length mark, accent, or orthographic sign, the actual symbol must be visibly present; reject empty parentheses, missing glyph placeholders, or explanations that refer to an absent sign. (3) representation consistency: do not mix IPA, phonemic notation, romanization, transliteration, and learner respelling; use one declared convention per field and reject pseudo-IPA or malformed symbols. (4) pragmatic translation: translate the communicative force, not just surface words; question tags, politeness, requests, offers, discourse particles, honorifics, and formulaic responses must sound natural in the instructional language while preserving the original function. (5) absolute-claim calibration: words equivalent to always, never, must, only, completely, or without exception require an actually exceptionless rule; otherwise use scoped wording such as generally, normally, in this pattern, or in standard usage. (6) exception safety: irregular lexicalized forms, counters, readings, spelling conventions, and historical forms must not be generalized into productive rules or described as impossible alternatives without strong support. (7) table integrity: term, pronunciation, meaning, target-language example, and translation must correspond to the same lexical item and sense; no row may contain a translation or pronunciation belonging to another item. (8) dialogue naturalness: every turn must be pragmatically plausible in sequence; pronouns, demonstratives, politeness level, speaker roles, and reference tracking must remain coherent from one turn to the next.
Calibrate difficulty genuinely: A1-A2 should use high-frequency concrete language and transparent explanations; B1-B2 may add productive variation, discourse functions, collocations, and nuanced grammar; C1-C2 may test fine register, pragmatics, stylistic contrast, idiomatic nuance, and discourse-level control. Every question must depend on taught language knowledge, have one defensible answer, and use plausible same-category distractors without commonsense, trivia, length, script, register, or category giveaways. Keep cultural notes neutral and accurately scoped, preserve source fidelity in source-based lessons, and keep terminology, translation senses, pronunciation systems, and grammatical claims consistent across pages. When a fine-grained claim is uncertain, simplify or remove it rather than guessing.
